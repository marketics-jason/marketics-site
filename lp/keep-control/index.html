Most owners think they have two options. There's a third.

You already know what's wrong. The reports arrive late. Nobody can tell you why last September was soft. The rate hasn't moved in four months. And you're paying 20–35% of everything the property earns for it.

So you look at leaving, and you see two doors.

Door one

Hire a different property manager. New name, same structure, same percentage, and you spend six months finding out whether this one is better.

Door two

Take it all back yourself. Cleaners, guest messages, maintenance calls, the calendar, the pricing. You bought a property. Now you own a job.

Neither of those is what you wanted. What you wanted was for the property to earn what it should without becoming your life.

Your property manager is doing two different jobs. Only one of them is worth a percentage.

Operations is the physical work. Cleaning, turnovers, guest messages, maintenance. That work costs roughly the same whether your revenue is flat or up 40%. It is a fixed cost.

Revenue is the commercial work. What you charge on a Tuesday in October. Where your listing sits against the twelve properties that come up beside it. Which guest the property is actually for.

Bundled together, you pay a percentage of everything for a service that is mostly fixed cost, and the revenue work, the only part that scales with what you earn, arrives as an afterthought inside a bundle.

Hotels have never confused these. Housekeeping and commercial strategy are separate functions, run by different people, paid differently. Short-term rentals bundled them because it was simpler to sell.

You can unbundle them. Most owners don't know that.

You keep everything except the pricing decisions.

Questions owners ask before they move.

Do I have to fire my property manager?

No. If your manager handles operations well, keep them for operations. Many owners keep their existing cleaning and guest-handling arrangement and change only who makes the pricing decisions.

Who controls my listing and my calendar?

You do. Your accounts stay in your name, your payouts go to your bank, and you keep the final word on your property. We work inside your accounts with your permission and you can revoke it at any time.

What if I'm under contract with my current manager?

Start with the audit anyway. It costs nothing and it tells you what the gap is worth, which is the number you need before you have any conversation about notice periods.

How is this different from a pricing tool?

A tool gives you a rate suggestion. It doesn't decide what your property is for, where it sits against its comp set, or when the market is turning. We use tools: the pricing tool is one input, not the service.

What does it cost?

10% of your net payout, taken per booking. No monthly fee, no retainer, nothing up front. It is split automatically at each booking payout, so there is never an invoice. A $500 commitment deposit at onboarding is refunded automatically when you complete the 90-day program.

What if my revenue doesn't go up?

There is no contract, so you can end it at any time. Our fee is a share of your revenue, which means our income only grows when yours does. And every property is audited before any target is set, so we don't take on a property where we don't see a gap worth closing.

Which markets do you work in?

Any market. The method travels. Over the past decade we've optimized 1,000+ listings across 22 markets.

How long before I see anything change?

Positioning changes show up in impressions within weeks. Revenue follows the booking window in your market, which is usually one to three months out. The audit will tell you what yours looks like.
