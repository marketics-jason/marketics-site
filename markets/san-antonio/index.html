Market Overview

A Market That Compounds.

San Antonio is one of the most durable short-term rental markets in Texas — and one of the most misunderstood. It doesn't spike the way event-driven markets do. It compounds. A steady stream of new residents, 30+ million annual visitors, and a tourism core anchored by the River Walk, the Pearl, and the Alamo give it a demand floor that most markets envy. But that same stability is why so many listings underperform: owners assume a good location does the work, and the numbers quietly drift.

The Opportunity

Why San Antonio Rewards Optimization.

The market has grown fast enough to get competitive. The city has been issuing well over a hundred new short-term rental permits a month, which means supply is climbing even as demand stays strong. In a market like that, being listed is no longer enough — the listings that win are the ones structured to surface in search, priced to the actual shape of local demand, and positioned to convert the click.

San Antonio's demand has a distinct rhythm worth pricing around. Peak season runs March through July, with April the strongest month of the year on the back of Fiesta San Antonio. Conventions, Spurs games, and festivals create sharp mid-week and event-driven spikes that flat pricing leaves on the table. The low season — roughly November through January — is where undifferentiated listings feel the softness most. Across the market, average daily rates sit in the high-$100s and have been trending up, with occupancy in the high-50s to low-60s percent. Those are market averages, not ceilings — the gap between an average listing and an optimized one is precisely where revenue is won or lost.

Regulation

Read This Before You Buy.

San Antonio is genuinely one of the more permissive major Texas markets — it explicitly permits short-term rentals citywide, with manageable permitting fees and clear tax and safety rules. Permits are valid for three years, which makes the market stable to operate in once you're in.

The caveat that catches first-time buyers: Type 2 short-term rentals — non-owner-occupied properties, which is what most investors buy — face density caps. The city limits Type 2 STRs to a percentage of units per block face and enforces minimum separation distances between them. In practice, a property that looks perfect on paper can be blocked simply because the block has already hit its cap. Confirm Type 2 eligibility for the specific address before purchase, not after — it's the single most common avoidable mistake in this market.

Execution

What "Optimized" Looks Like Here.

Optimization in San Antonio is rarely one lever. It's the sequence: making sure the listing surfaces in search at all, earning the click with the first photo and the first two lines, converting that click with honest positioning, and then pricing dynamically against the right comparable set — Fiesta and convention weeks priced differently than a soft January Tuesday. Miss any one of those and the property underperforms a market that should carry it.
