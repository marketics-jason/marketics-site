Why isn't my Airbnb getting bookings?

There are five reasons a listing stops booking. Only one of them is price — and it's the one almost everyone reaches for first.

Jason Baxter · Founder, Marketics · Operating since 2015 · LinkedIn ↗ · Published July 14, 2026 · Updated July 14, 2026
The short answer

When a short-term rental stops getting bookings, there are five common causes, and they require completely different fixes. (1) Visibility — the listing isn't being shown, usually a ranking problem. (2) Conversion — it's being shown but not clicked, almost always the photos or the title. (3) Positioning — it's being clicked but not booked, because it's landing on the wrong shortlist and losing every comparison. (4) Seasonality — demand genuinely dropped, and no listing change will manufacture guests who aren't looking. (5) Price — the rate is genuinely wrong for the tier. Most owners diagnose the problem as (5) because it's the only lever that's easy to pull. Cutting the rate when the real problem is (1), (2), or (3) doesn't fix anything — it just lowers what you earn on the bookings you were already going to get. Diagnose before you discount.

Diagnose before you discount.

There's one number that tells you where the problem is, and most owners never look at it: the ratio of views to bookings.

  • → Few views → visibility problem. You're not being shown.
  • → Views but no clicks → conversion problem. You're being shown and skipped.
  • → Clicks but no bookings → positioning problem. You're being considered and rejected.

Three completely different diseases. One symptom. If you don't know which one you have, you cannot know which fix to apply — and the price slider is the wrong answer to two of the three.

Cause 1: Visibility. Nobody's seeing it.

If impressions are low, the listing is ranked poorly — and ranking is downstream of past performance: booking velocity, review count and recency, response rate, cancellation history, calendar availability, and how complete the listing is.

What actually helps

Improving the signals the ranking reads. Fill the calendar (even at a lower rate, briefly, to rebuild velocity — this is the one legitimate use of a price cut). Respond instantly. Keep the calendar open and current. Get reviews moving again.

What doesn't

Cutting the rate on a listing nobody is seeing. You cannot discount your way out of invisibility.

Cause 2: Conversion. They see it and scroll past.

Good impressions, poor click-through. This is almost always the first photo. Guests are scrolling a grid — your hero image is competing against seven others, and the decision takes under a second.

What actually helps

Professional photography, shot and sequenced correctly. The hero image is the whole job. Then the title, then the first line.

What doesn't

Lowering the price. A cheaper listing with a bad photo is still a bad photo.

Cause 3: Positioning. They click, then book someone else.

They found you, they looked, they chose another property. That means you're on the wrong shortlist — being compared against properties you can't beat, or being presented as something you're not.

What actually helps

An honest read of which tier the property genuinely belongs in, and the presentation work to land it there. Sometimes that's up. Sometimes it's a right-sized reset.

What doesn't

Discounting into a comparison you were losing on quality. You'll win a few bookings from guests who wanted the other property and settled for yours — and those guests write the three-star reviews.

Cause 4: Seasonality. It's not you.

Sometimes demand simply isn't there. November in a summer market is not a listing problem, and no amount of optimization manufactures guests who aren't searching.

What actually helps

Adjusting minimum-stay rules, targeting a different guest type (remote workers, mid-term stays, local staycations), and being honest with yourself about the shape of the year.

What doesn't

Panic-discounting into a trough. You don't win the bookings — there are none to win — and you've now anchored your rate lower going into your strong season, which is where the money actually was.

Cause 5: Price. Sometimes it really is the rate.

It does happen. If the property is genuinely priced above what its tier supports — the photos are good, the positioning is honest, the visibility is fine, and it still isn't booking — then the rate is wrong, and it should come down.

But notice how far down the list this is. Price is the last thing to check, because it's the only lever that costs you money permanently. Everything above it is a one-time investment that pays forward. A price cut compounds against you: it lowers every future night, anchors the market's expectation, and teaches the platform your property is worth less.

Cut the price and you pay for it forever. Raise the value and you pay once.

Key takeaways

  • → Five causes: visibility, conversion, positioning, seasonality, price. They need different fixes.
  • → The views-to-bookings ratio tells you which one you have. Look at it before you touch anything.
  • → Few views = ranking. Views, no clicks = photos. Clicks, no bookings = wrong shortlist.
  • → Price is the last thing to check — it's the only fix that costs you permanently.
  • → Most owners reach for the price slider first because it's the easiest lever, not the right one.
